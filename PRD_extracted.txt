1. Executive Summary

AgriChain Connect is an end-to-end digital marketplace and supply chain operating system that digitizes the traditional agricultural trade chain: Farmer → Local Aggregator → Wholesaler → Processor/Manufacturer → Distributor → Retailer. The platform gives every participant in this chain expanded market access, price transparency, secure payments, and AI-assisted route optimization — with the explicit goal of increasing the price realization for upstream sellers (farmers, aggregators) while reducing inefficiency, risk, and predatory practices across the chain.

Each of the six supply chain roles gets a purpose-built interface reflecting their actual buying/selling behavior — for example, a Farmer only sells, while an Aggregator, Wholesaler, and Distributor both buy and sell. The platform is multilingual (English, Hindi, Punjabi, Marathi), supports escrow-based and cash-based payments, includes a negotiation/counter-offer system, and enforces transparency through a mandatory ₹100 value-distribution disclosure visible to all roles.

1.1 Problem Statement

Farmers and small aggregators are structurally price-takers with little visibility into downstream margins or alternative buyers.

Multiple manual, cash-heavy intermediary layers add cost and delay without adding proportional value.

Trust and payment risk (delayed payments, predatory load rejections, price renegotiation at the gate) are widespread and unaddressed by any centralized reputation system.

Language and digital literacy barriers exclude a large share of rural users from existing agri-tech platforms.

There is no dynamic, data-driven way to identify when a traditional intermediary tier should be bypassed in favor of a more efficient, trustworthy, better-paying node.

1.2 Solution Summary

A single mobile/web application with six role-specific dashboards mapped 1:1 to the real-world supply chain roles.

A trust score system (0–100) computed from payment reliability, fulfillment behavior, punctuality, and peer reviews, gating who the AI routing engine will recommend.

An AI Route Optimization Engine that can skip a low-trust or below-market-price intermediary tier and route the transaction directly to the next healthy tier.

Escrow wallet, UPI/bank transfer, and logged cash payments, plus a structured negotiation and counter-offer workflow between any two connected parties.

A ₹100 value-distribution transparency panel shown identically (with role-relevant highlighting) across all six interfaces.

Adaptive nearby-member discovery with automatic radius expansion so a user is never shown "no members found."

Full support for English, Hindi, Marathi, and Punjabi from first launch, with language persisted to the user's account.

Build a defensible data asset (transaction history, trust scores, live price index) that improves routing quality over time.

5. Payments, Escrow & Negotiation

5.1 Payment Methods

Method

Description

Key Requirements

Escrow Wallet

Platform holds buyer's funds after order confirmation and releases to seller upon agreed trigger (e.g., delivery confirmation or inspection window expiry).

Configurable hold period; auto-release rules; dispute-hold capability; full ledger per transaction.

UPI / Bank Transfer

Direct digital transfer between buyer and seller, tracked by the platform for record-keeping and Trust Score input.

5.1.1 Escrow Wallet — Functional Detail

Buyer funds the escrow wallet when placing/accepting an order (or a deposit percentage, if partial escrow is supported).

Funds are held in escrow through the order lifecycle (see Section 9, Order Status Tracking).

Release trigger: automatic on "Delivered" confirmation by buyer, OR manual release by buyer, OR automatic release after a configurable inspection window (e.g., 48 hours) with no dispute raised.

If a dispute is raised before release, funds remain held pending resolution (dispute-resolution workflow to be defined in a future phase).

All escrow events (funded, held, released, disputed, refunded) are logged and visible to both parties and factored into the Payment Reliability & Speed pillar of the Trust Score.

5.1.2 Cash Payment Logging

Seller marks "Cash Payment Received" with amount and date.

Buyer must confirm the same amount and date.

If both confirmations match, the order is marked Paid; if they mismatch, the order is flagged for both parties to resolve, and repeated mismatches negatively affect Trust Score.

Cash-paid orders still appear in the ₹100 value-distribution and transaction history for transparency purposes, but carry a "Cash — Self-Reported" indicator.

5.2 Negotiation & Counter-Offer System

Every buy/sell relationship on the platform — Farmer↔Aggregator, Aggregator↔Wholesaler, Wholesaler↔Processor, Processor↔Distributor, Distributor↔Retailer — supports structured negotiation, not just accept/reject.

5.2.1 Negotiation Flow

Seller creates a listing with an asking price (per unit) and quantity.

Buyer views listing and either (a) accepts at asking price, or (b) submits a counter-offer with proposed price, quantity, and optional message/pickup terms.

Seller can accept the counter-offer, reject it, or submit a further counter-offer. This continues as a threaded negotiation with full history retained.

Either party can set an expiry on an offer (e.g., valid for 6 hours) given the perishability of agricultural goods.

Once accepted, the offer converts into a confirmed order and enters the Order Status Tracking flow (Section 9).

5.2.2 Negotiation UI Requirements

Threaded, chat-like interface showing full offer/counter-offer history with timestamps.

Live comparison against the Live Market Index Price so both parties can see if an offer is above/below market.

Visible Trust Score of the counterparty at every step of the negotiation.

Push/in-app notification on every new counter-offer or expiring offer.

6. ₹100 Value Distribution Transparency Feature

To build trust and demonstrate fairness, every one of the six role interfaces must display a standardized panel showing how ₹100 of final consumer value is distributed across all six supply chain tiers for the relevant crop/product category. This is a platform-wide transparency commitment, not a per-role customization.

6.1 Requirements

The ₹100 breakdown must be shown using the same underlying dataset across all six interfaces — a Farmer and a Retailer looking at the same crop category should see identical numbers.

Values should be computed from actual platform transaction data where available (per crop category / region), falling back to published market/industry benchmark data where platform data is insufficient.

The panel should visually highlight the viewing user's own tier (e.g., a Farmer's panel highlights the "Farmer" segment) while still showing all six segments for context.

Should be filterable by crop/product category, since distribution varies (e.g., fresh produce vs. processed goods).

Should be accessible from the dashboard at all times, not buried in settings — this is a core trust-building feature.

8. AI Route Optimization Engine

The AI Route Optimization Engine dynamically maps the most profitable, secure, and efficient transaction path for an upstream seller (Farmer, Aggregator, Wholesaler, Processor) to a downstream buyer, skipping inefficient or high-risk intermediary tiers when justified. Retailer and end consumer are out of scope for routing recommendations (Retailer is a terminal buy-only node).

8.1 Baseline Chain Structure

Farmer → Aggregator → Wholesaler → Processor → Distributor  (Retailer and end consumer are outside the routing engine's scope.)

8.2 Dynamic Node Evaluation Logic

For every transaction step, the engine evaluates the immediate next sequential tier (Node N+1) against three strict, ordered filtering constraints:

#

Constraint

Check

Pass Condition

1

Availability Check

Does a verified buyer exist at the next tier within a viable geographic/logistics radius?

Yes

2

Trust Score Threshold

Does the buyer meet the platform's minimum required Trust Score?

≥ 4.0 / 5.0 (≥ 80 / 100)

3

Market Price Fairness

Is the buyer's offered price within acceptable variance of the Live Market Index Price?

No lower than −5% of market average

8.3 Skip-Node Routing Rules

8.3.1 Standard Route

If the next sequential tier passes all three checks, the engine routes the transaction to that tier normally — no bypass.

8.3.2 Skip-Node Route (Bypass Protocol)

If the next sequential tier fails any one of the three checks (unavailable, low trust, or predatory/low pricing), the engine triggers the Bypassing Protocol.

The failing tier is completely dropped from the recommended route, and the engine recalculates logistics directly to the next healthy tier (Node N+2).

Example: If an Aggregator's nearby Wholesaler network has poor trust scores or below-market buy rates, the engine recalculates transport/logistics cost and redirects the recommended sale directly to a verified Processor, skipping the Wholesaler tier entirely.

If Node N+2 also fails, the engine continues evaluating Node N+3, and so on, until a passing node is found or the chain is exhausted (in which case the standard route is shown with a warning, not silently withheld).

8.4 Output Generation Requirements

For a given user and transaction context, the engine must output all three of the following:

The Optimized Route — the exact sequential chain of entities the user is recommended to sell to (or buy from), from current tier to the recommended destination tier.

Skipped Nodes Justification — a clear, plain-language, localized reason for every bypassed tier (e.g., "Wholesaler skipped due to low price anomaly — offered price was 9% below the live market index, exceeding the −5% threshold.")

Net Profitability Analysis — a side-by-side comparison showing that the recommended direct route covers any extra transport/logistics cost incurred by skipping a tier and yields a higher net return than the standard route through the unhealthy node.

8.5 UI Requirements for Route Recommendations

Displayed as a dedicated "AI Route Recommendations" card/screen within the dashboards of Farmer, Aggregator, Wholesaler, Processor, and Distributor (the five roles that sell to a next tier).

Route shown as a visual chain/path (e.g., You → [Skipped: Wholesaler] → Processor), with the skipped tier visually struck through or marked, not simply omitted, so the user understands why.

Net Profitability Analysis shown as a simple comparison table or chart: Standard Route Net Return vs. Optimized Route Net Return, including transport cost deltas.

Tapping a skipped node reveals the specific justification text and the underlying failed constraint(s).

User retains full control — the AI recommendation is advisory; the user can still choose to sell to the standard next-tier node manually if they prefer.

All route/justification text must be localized per Section 7.

8.6 Data Dependencies

Live Market Index Price feed, per crop/product category and region.

Real-time buyer availability within an expandable geographic radius (shared logic with Adaptive Nearby Member Discovery, Section 10).

Live Trust Scores for all candidate nodes (Section 9).

Logistics/transport cost estimation model to support the Net Profitability Analysis.

9. Trust Score System

Every registered user, regardless of role, has a single Trust Score that is computed continuously from platform transaction data. It is the primary risk/quality signal used by the AI Route Optimization Engine and is visible to any counterparty considering a transaction.

9.1 Scale & Threshold

Scale: 0 to 100 points, also displayed as 0.0 to 5.0 stars for readability.

System default threshold: 80 points (4.0 stars).

Any user falling below the threshold is automatically flagged "High Risk" and is bypassed by the Route Optimization Engine's recommendations (though not blocked from the platform entirely — they can still transact, just without being the AI's recommended route).

9.3 Functional Requirements

Trust Score recalculates after every completed transaction (or on a rolling/batched basis, e.g., nightly — implementation detail for engineering, but must reflect data within 24 hours).

Score history should be viewable by the user themselves (trend over time), with pillar-level breakdown so users understand what to improve.

Counterparties see the aggregate score/star rating and pillar summary of any user they are negotiating or transacting with — full granular history is private to the account owner.

New users start with a neutral/provisional score (exact starting value and "cold start" handling to be defined with data/eng team) and a visible "New Member" indicator until a minimum transaction count is reached.

"High Risk" flag is visible on the user's profile card wherever it is shown platform-wide (negotiation threads, nearby-member lists, route recommendations).

Peer reviews (Pillar 4) can only be submitted by users who completed an actual transaction with the reviewed party, to prevent fake/retaliatory reviews.

10. Smart Nearby Member Location & Adaptive Discovery

Feature name: Adaptive Nearby Member Discovery. The platform uses the requesting user's location to identify and display the nearest available members in the relevant next (or previous) supply-chain tier — transporters, buyers, warehouses, cold storage providers, or other relevant chain members — with automatic radius expansion so a user is never shown a dead-end "No members found" result.

10.1 Core Logic

Nearby Members First: the system searches for available, qualifying members within a predefined nearby radius (e.g., 10 km — exact default to be tuned per region density).

Smart Radius Expansion: if no suitable member is available nearby, the system automatically expands the search radius in steps (e.g., 10 km → 25 km → 50 km → beyond) until qualifying members are found.

Distance-Based Ranking: all returned members are ranked nearest-to-farthest so the user can weigh convenience against other factors (price, trust score).

Availability Check: only members who are currently marked available and capable of handling the specific crop/request/volume are shown.

Location Transparency: the user can view the approximate distance and location of each recommended member before selecting one.

10.2 Example

If no transporter or buyer is available within 10 km, the system automatically expands the search to 25 km, then 50 km, and beyond — always surfacing the nearest available options instead of returning an empty result.

10.3 UI Requirements

Combined map + list view: pins on a map plus a scrollable, distance-sorted list, each entry showing name, role, distance, Trust Score, and availability status.

Clear visual indicator when the system has expanded beyond the default radius (e.g., "Showing results up to 45 km — no closer matches available") so users understand why farther options appear.

Filter/sort controls: by distance, Trust Score, or price where applicable.

Reused across all relevant flows: Farmer finding buyers, Aggregator/Wholesaler/Processor/Distributor finding both upstream suppliers and downstream buyers, and Retailer finding Distributors.

10.4 Data Dependencies

User-level geolocation (opt-in, with manual address fallback for users who decline device location access).

Live availability status per member (must be kept current — e.g., via an active/inactive toggle or automatic timeout).

Shared with the AI Route Optimization Engine's Availability Check constraint (Section 8.2).

11.3.2 Login

User authenticates with Username + Password.

On invalid credentials, the system shows a generic error — "Invalid username or password. Please check your credentials and try again" — and must not reveal whether the username exists separately from whether the password was wrong (prevents username enumeration).

11.4 Role Persistence & Security Requirement

On every login, the backend retrieves the user's stored role from the database and routes them to the corresponding role-specific dashboard — the user is never asked to re-select their role.

Critical security requirement: the frontend's role selection screen is used only during onboarding/registration and for navigation convenience. It must never be treated as proof of a user's identity or role for any authenticated action. All role-based access control decisions must be made server-side from the authenticated user's stored account record.

12. Order Status Tracking

A visual, real-time order status bar is displayed on the order details page for every order, letting Farmers and all other supply-chain members easily track progress at a glance.

12.1 Order Status Flow

Order Placed → Confirmed → Packed → Shipped/Dispatched → Out for Delivery → Delivered

Example rendering: ✓ Order Placed → ✓ Confirmed → ✓ Packed → ● Shipped → ○ Out for Delivery → ○ Delivered

12.2 Requirements

The current stage is clearly highlighted; completed stages are visually marked (e.g., checkmark) so users can understand progress at a glance.

The date and time of each status update is displayed.

An estimated delivery date is shown when available.

The progress bar updates automatically the moment the seller or delivery/logistics partner changes the order status — no manual refresh needed.

Applies uniformly to every role's order views — a Farmer tracking a sale to an Aggregator sees the same style of bar as a Distributor tracking a delivery to a Retailer.

Status changes should trigger localized push/in-app notifications to both parties.

Escrow release logic (Section 5.1.1) and Trust Score's Operational Punctuality pillar (Section 9.2) both consume these status timestamps as inputs.

13. Design & Prototyping Process

Once this PRD's role-specific interface structure (Section 4) is confirmed by stakeholders, the visual UI for each of the six role dashboards — plus the shared components (Sections 5–10 and 12) — will be designed in Stitch. Suggested sequencing:

Design the shared component library first: navigation shell, Trust Score badge, negotiation thread, wallet/payment module, order status bar, ₹100 transparency panel, nearby-member map/list.

Design the Farmer and Final Retailer dashboards next, as the two single-sided (simplest) interfaces — useful for validating the shared components.

Design the four dual-sided dashboards (Aggregator, Wholesaler, Processor, Distributor), reusing the buy-side and sell-side patterns established in step 2.

Design the onboarding flow (language → role → login/register) and AI Route Recommendation screens last, since they depend on final visual language from the dashboards.

All screens must be designed in at least English and Hindi to validate text-expansion/layout issues early, before Punjabi and Marathi are added.

14. Assumptions, Open Questions & Risks

14.1 Assumptions

A reliable source for the Live Market Index Price (per crop/region) is available or will be built/sourced (e.g., government mandi price APIs, internal transaction-derived pricing).

Users have (or can be onboarded onto) smartphones with basic data connectivity; the app should still degrade gracefully in low-connectivity rural areas.

A payment gateway partner supporting UPI/NEFT/IMPS/RTGS and escrow-style fund holding is available for integration.

Stitch will be used for high-fidelity UI design following sign-off on this PRD's structural requirements.

14.2 Open Questions for Stakeholders

What is the exact default "nearby radius" and expansion step sequence for Adaptive Nearby Member Discovery — should this vary by crop perishability or region (urban vs. rural density)?

What is the dispute-resolution process when an escrow payment is disputed — manual platform arbitration, automated rules, or a hybrid?

What is the "cold start" Trust Score for brand-new users, and how many transactions are needed before a score is considered reliable enough to be shown/used in routing?

Should the ₹100 value-distribution figures be sourced live from platform transaction data from day one, or seeded with external benchmark data until sufficient platform data exists?

Is voice input/output for low-literacy users an in-scope v1 requirement or a fast-follow v1.1 enhancement?

What regulatory/compliance requirements apply to holding farmer/trader funds in an escrow wallet (e.g., RBI payment aggregator/escrow regulations in India)?
